## The question

§4 (`drafts/mod-head.tex`, from "For PHP (p. 584)") compares the heads that take no determiner (pronouns, proper nouns, the compounds, and simple determinatives standing alone) on three questions:
- whether such a head takes a pre-head dependent at all;
- whether it takes a non-restrictive adjective;
- whether a restrictive adjective needs a determiner.

It concludes that only fusion's adjacency predicts *\*Remaining few were the hangers-on* beside *The remaining few were the hangers-on*.

This note takes up the cells §4 left open, in the exclamation with *lucky*, *poor*, and *silly* (*Lucky you!*). The question is whether the pre-head restriction separates the analyses, and whether fusion's derivation holds across the words CGEL fuses.

## Three accounts going in

**Fusion's adjacency.** CGEL derives the compounds' gap from fusion: "Because determiner and head are fused it is not possible for these forms to take internal pre-head dependents" (p. 423). The same derivation covers simple determinatives standing alone. It predicts that every fused determiner–head, compound or simple, refuses a pre-head adjective.

**Ordinary Head with a pronoun-type statement.** Pronouns "do not normally allow internal pre-head dependents", with an exception for "a few adjectives such as *lucky*, *poor*, *silly* with the core personal pronouns" (p. 430). Ordinary Head's own condition, "only after a determiner" (Table 1), excludes a pre-head adjective with a bare simple determinative. Table 1 puts *everybody* and *everyone* under the pronouns' statement and its exception.

**A semantic condition (my hypothesis, not CGEL's).** The adjective is "semantically non-restrictive" (p. 430), so the exclamation might need a definite or total referent. If so, *Lucky few!* would be out because *few* is indefinite, and it would be evidence for neither analysis.

## What the results show

**The exclamation takes every kind of bare head except one.** It takes:
- personal pronouns, in every person and number;
- names;
- common nouns, singular count and plural;
- the personal compounds.

It rejects simple determinatives standing alone, definite or not.

**The semantic account fails.** Definite *both* is out and indefinite *somebody* is in.

**Number doesn't explain it.** Plural pronouns are in.

**Both analyses predicted *\*Lucky few!* and *\*Lucky both!*.** Fusion predicted them by adjacency, ordinary Head by its condition. So the exclusion itself doesn't separate the two. The test bears on the comparison in two narrower ways.
- **Ordinary Head's condition doesn't behave like the pronoun restriction it was modelled on.** §4 calls ordinary Head's condition "a further statement of a kind the grammar already contains", the pronouns' restriction (p. 430). In the exclamation, the pronouns' restriction gives way (*Lucky you!*), and so does the requirement that a singular count noun take a determiner (*Poor man!*). Ordinary Head's condition doesn't give way. So ordinary Head needs its condition to hold where the pronoun restriction doesn't. On fusion that difference follows from adjacency: the determinative is the determiner, and nothing in the NP comes before the determiner.
- **Fusion's derivation fails for the compounds.** PHP say a condition like ordinary Head's is "precisely the kind of stipulation which is not required when the determiner and head functions are fused" (p. 584, quoted in §4, said of the compounds). *Poor everybody!* and *Poor somebody!* mean fusion needs an exception for the compounds anyway. Its ways out are the ones §4 lists: conversion, a listed exception, or an adjective modifying the whole NP. Ordinary Head doesn't derive these either; it gets them by putting the compounds under the pronouns' statement and its exception.

**One assumption: the exclamation has no silent determiner.** If it had one, *few* in *Lucky few!* would follow a determiner, and both analyses would allow it, as they allow *the lucky few*. CGEL gives two precedents for treating such NPs as having no determiner:
- in *Lucky you!*, *lucky* is a pre-head dependent of a pronoun (p. 430), and pronouns "do not permit determiners" (p. 429);
- vocatives take bare singular count nouns with a pre-head adjective (*young man*, *old chap*, pp. 522–523).

The vocative is a different function from this exclamation, but CGEL treats both as NPs with no determiner, not with a silent one.

## Where the data point

Fusion's adjacency holds for the simple determinatives standing alone, in the restrictive case (*\*Remaining few*) and in the exclamation. It fails for the compounds, which pattern with pronouns in the exclamation.

One candidate for the split is the compounds' personal nominal base. CGEL describes them as "the morphological compounding of a determinative base with a nominal one", with ·*body* and ·*one* "personal" (p. 423). The simple determinatives get their 'people' reading only by convention (p. 414).

The grammar that fits these results is a further point in the family: Det–Head for simple determinatives standing alone, and ordinary heads for the compounds. That runs against CGEL's morphological case for fusing the compounds (p. 423). It rests on one test: §3 finds the compounds' restrictor neutral, since fusion is neither necessary nor sufficient for its position. It also rests on one speaker's judgments and two COCA tokens for the compounds. So it's where these data point, not a conclusion.

## For §6 and the drafts (edits wait on how §6 states its verdict on determiner–head fusion)

The fact fusion derives is firmer, and the derivation covers less than CGEL claims.
- **§4** would give the exclamation as a test that fails the semantic account and fails fusion's derivation for the compounds. It would say that ordinary Head's condition holds where the pronoun restriction gives way. Its "simple determinatives don't seem to" can be firmer.
- **Table 1's exclamation row** would cover *somebody* as well as *everybody* and *everyone*, and would say the construction also takes bare common nouns.
- **§6** weighs a firmer derived fact, and a weaker case for ordinary Head's condition being of a kind the grammar already has, against the preference for simpler representations and the compound exception.

## What the judgments can't settle

CGEL's plural example *\*I met interesting them all* (p. 430) shows that the pronoun restriction isn't an effect of countability. So a pronoun statement widened to cover determinatives standing alone would also predict *\*Remaining few*:
- fusion predicts it with no widening;
- ordinary Head predicts it with a widening chosen to fit *few*.

Whether the simple determinatives belong with pronouns on independent grounds is a question about categories.

**Cross-paper.** The determinatives-as-nouns paper pairs determinatives-as-nouns with ordinary Head. These results support ordinary heads for the compounds but cut against them for the simple determinatives standing alone, which are the words §6 is about. That paper's comparison of ordinary Head with fusion (its section "Ordinary Head, fusion, and modifier attachment") will need to take account of this.

Not covered here: whether adjacency predicts anything beyond pre-head modifiers (the reversal question for §6), and cardinals.
