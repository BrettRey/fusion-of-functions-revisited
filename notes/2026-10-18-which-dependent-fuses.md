# Which dependent fuses? A principle for (3a, b)
<!-- SUMMARY: reserve argument for a referee, not in the paper (Brett, 18 Oct 2026): CGEL's adjacency definition (p. 410) picks the fused dependent in *the other two* but not in *the many who did*; Brett's drop test (\*the who live there) settles it; needed equally by ordinary Head, so it doesn't separate the analyses; no counterexample found, COCA `the who/which [vv*]` included (19 Oct) · status: note · updated: 2026-10-19 -->

## The question

In §4's (3a) *This copy is defective but [the other two] are fine*, why is *two* the fused modifier–head and not *other*? In (3b) *I respect the views of [the many who did]*, why *many* and not the relative clause *who did*? CGEL only asserts the first: "Much the most common case has a cardinal numeral as head, as in [i]" (p. 416). Brett: "CGEL owes us a principle."

## What doesn't work

- "The head follows its pre-head modifiers" and "a relative clause can't head a nominal" assume the answer (Brett: "assumes the consequent"). Under fusion a modifier *can* become a head, so calling the clause a modifier settles nothing.
- Number agreement: *The other is fine* / *The other two are fine* seems to put the plural on *two*, and *the many who were there* gives *who* a plural antecedent. But *the rich who live there* is plural with no plural ending anywhere (CGEL p. 418), so agreement shows the nominal's number, not which word heads it (Brett's counterexample).

## What does

1. **CGEL's own definition** (p. 410, checked in the PDF): fused-head NPs "are those where the head is combined with a dependent function that in ordinary NPs is adjacent to the head". In *the other two books*, *two* is next to *books* and *other* isn't, so (3a) is settled. In *the many people who did*, both *many* and *who did* are next to *people*, and CGEL allows fusion with an internal modifier, so (3b) isn't.
2. **Brett's drop test**: of the adjacent dependents, the one that fuses can itself stand after the determiner. *The many* is fine, \**the who live there* isn't; *the rich* is fine. It's a string test, not an appeal to which word is the head, so it isn't circular.

Combined: adjacency picks the candidates; the drop test picks among them. Ordinary Head needs the same two steps to say which word heads the nominal, so the principle doesn't separate the analyses.

## Counterexamples looked for (none found)

- Payne et al.'s *the very hard of hearing* (p. 580): a dependent that follows the head in the ordinary NP (*people very hard of hearing*) fuses. Consistent with the drop test (*the very hard of hearing* is fine), and it shows the principle can't be "pre-head dependents only".
- Payne et al.'s *no stopping him* (p. 600): a VP heads an NP with a determiner, so clause-like heads after a determiner aren't excluded in general; \**the who live there* is specific to relative clauses.
- Conversions (*the who and the why*, *a who's who*, *the have-nots*, *the over-sixties*): nouns with nominal morphology, not relative clauses heading a nominal.
- Archaic *the which*: a relative word with an antecedent, not headless.
- CGEL: no discussion of *the who*/*the which* as a fused head found (search of the text conversion for *the who|which|that* + verb).
- COCA `the who [vv*]` and `the which [vv*]`, run by hand by Brett on 19 Oct 2026: no relevant hits. A real counterexample would have been a relative clause with no head noun straight after *the*.
