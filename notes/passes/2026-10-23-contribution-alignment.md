## The claim, from the front and back matter alone

The question is what form evidence justifies giving a constituent a second function. Holding CGEL's commitments fixed, the paper answers it in three parts:
- no property of form shows the modifier function in Mod–Head;
- one property (no pre-head adjective) favours the determiner function in Det–Head, but the balance still favours ordinary Head;
- fused relatives show the function inside the clause in three properties, so a second function is favoured there (stated as fusion), though not forced.

The kind of claim is a conditional, comparative argument about which analysis to choose, with corpus evidence. It is not a new analysis of the data. Both analyses describe the same strings.

## The four questions

1. **Title.** *Fusion of functions revisited: When does a head have a second function?* names the topic and the paper's question. It describes this version (the criterion and its verdicts), not an earlier one. No change.
2. **Abstract against body.**
   - The body keeps every promise the abstract makes.
   - Two results are in the body but not the abstract. The first is the genitive: CGEL's own diagnostic divides the determinatives in a way their shared functions don't predict. The second is *once*, *twice*, and *thrice*. The abstract's header comment says both are left to the body by design (Brett's rewrite, 23 Oct), so neither is listed as a defect. The genitive is the stronger candidate if room is ever wanted: it's a negative result against CGEL's main argument for Det–Head.
   - **Proposal A1.** The abstract never says the result is conditional. §1, §2, and §8 all do ("On the commitments held fixed in Section 2"). A desk editor reading only the abstract could take "it doesn't justify the extra function" as unconditional. Possible repair, before "I argue": "Holding the grammar's other commitments fixed, I argue that …" (+6 words).
   - **Proposal A2.** "ordinarily no adjective comes before a bare determinative". *Ordinarily* gestures at the exclamations (*Poor everybody!*) without saying so, and Muse flagged it as puzzling to an abstract-only reader. Possible repair: "that no adjective comes before a bare determinative outside a few exclamations" (+3 words).
3. **Introduction.**
   - It says what problem the paper takes on ("what evidence could justify that doubling"), names the criterion, and says the result is conditional.
   - One sentence asserts more than the paper shows: "but the criterion can be applied to a second function in any analysis". Nothing in the paper applies it outside CGEL. Muse raised this (§1P4) and it was listed as not taken earlier today, so it's noted here only.
4. **Conclusion.** No overclaims found. Each claim in §8 matches a section's result, including "on the commitments held fixed" and "favours … without forcing it". After today's edits, its last two sentences separate the second function (which the evidence favours) from fusion (CGEL's statement of it).
