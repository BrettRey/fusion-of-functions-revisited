## The levels in this paper

The paper works at three levels and moves between them often:
- **corpus-distributional:** shares and counts in COCA, NOW, and iWeb;
- **judgment:** acceptability, CGEL's or Brett's;
- **analytical:** what each grammar has to state.

The interpretive level, what a phrase means, is deliberately excluded from the criterion ("Interpretation alone isn't form", §2). The audit asks whether each move between levels is marked.

## Check 4: mereology

The usual agents in this paper are analyses and grammars: "fusion derives", "ordinary Head states", "the head analysis needs", "either grammar keeps", "the grammar defended here". That is the field's standard idiom for what an analysis commits to, and none of them is credited with judging or knowing. "The evidence favours" and "the criterion asks for" are the same idiom. "The form answers to both functions" (L1) is the one that reads as the word doing something. Once "in usage" marks it as a correlation, it's the standard way of stating a case dependency.

No large internal map is offered as an explanation. The corpus tables are used to test the analyses' predictions, which is the right use.
