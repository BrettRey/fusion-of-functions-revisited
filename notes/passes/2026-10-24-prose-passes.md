## 4. Lakoffian metaphor

**Frames:**
1. **Accounting and balance.** "what the extra structure buys" (§2), "the trade" (§6), "saves" (§4, §6), "gains" (§6), "economies of the grammar" (§6), "weighed", "the balance favours" (§1, §6, §8), "advantage".
   - Entailment: a common currency, so derivations, statements and functions can be traded against each other.
   - The paper surfaces that assumption itself. "economies of the grammar, not facts of form" and "A reader who weights derivation above parsimony will take fusion …" (§6) concede that the weights depend on the reader.
   - So the frame is staged, not smuggled. No change.
2. **Law (burden, licence, permission).** "I put the burden on the second function" (§1, §2) and "The burden … decides it" (§6).
   - The burden-of-proof frame decides close cases, but §2 argues for where it lies (PHP call the second function "the major departure").
   - *Licence* and *permission* are a deliberate contrast (fusion and ordinary Head), checked in tonight's terminology pass.
   - No change.
3. **Visibility.** "where the second function shows" (§7 heading); "the second function shows in form" (§1); "shows the modifier function" (§3, §4, §8).
   - Entailment: the function exists and the question is only whether it's visible. That fits §§3–4, where "nothing … shows the modifier function" is a negative.
   - In §7 it presupposes what the section, after today's D2 edits, only favours. Muse raised the same register tension (R2, minor).
   - **Proposal M1:** change the §7 heading to "Fused relatives: evidence for a second function" (same length). Leave §1's "shows in form, twice categorically and once as a preference" alone. There the claim is that the evidence shows, and the rhythm is worth keeping.
4. **Tree terms (parent, mother).** "Mothers" appears only in PHP's quotation. "Parents" follows the house rule (parent/child/sibling). No change.

**Mixed frames:** "The burden … decides it: I take the balance to favour ordinary Head" (§6) joins law and scales. The combination is coherent (the burden tips a close balance) and reads as intended.

**"Load-bearing":** none in the paper. The cap is unused.
