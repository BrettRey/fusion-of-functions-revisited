## Lobeck 1995, ch. 3, "Two Functional Heads in English DP", pp. 71–99 (DOI 10.1093/oso/9780195091816.003.0004)

- **Licensing condition** (2), p. 71: "An empty, non-arbitrary pronominal must be properly head-governed, and governed by an X-0 specified for strong agreement." The condition is stated for *non-arbitrary* null pronominals, so antecedentless human readings (*few would dare*) are outside it.
- **The problem she sets out to solve** is ours, p. 71: the single-DET analysis "fails to explain why more than one determiner element can apparently precede a nominal projection" (*John's many books*, *The six women*, *These few articles*). Her (4b), p. 72: "Many students enrolled in the class, but [the few [e] who dropped it later] said it was too difficult." (antecedent *students*).
- **Two heads**, p. 80: "definite elements in English, including demonstratives, the definite article the, and definite quantifiers such as all/both/each/every, fill DET. NUM, on the other hand, is the position of the indefinite article a, numerals, and indefinite quantifiers, including few/many/some/several." Empty NP is licensed by a filled NUM [+Plural] (p. 87, p. 91: *many/few/six [e] left disappointed*, (68)); *the [e]* is out because *the* lacks agreement (p. 88, (55)); *the two [e]* is fine (p. 89, (57)).
- **[+Partitive]** is added to rescue *each* and *one*: with [−Plural] alone "we expect, contrary to fact, ellipsis to be ungrammatical in all cases" (p. 93). "Suppose, then, that [+Partitive] is a feature for which quantifiers can be specified, and that every, but not, for example, each, lacks the feature" (p. 94). *No* is "like the, lacking strong agreement" (p. 98 n. 9); *every one [e]* "may be an exceptional case" (p. 99 n. 10).
- **Bearing.**
  - *The some who*: *some* sits in NUM with *few* (p. 80), and *the* in DET. So her structure generates *the some [e] who* exactly as it generates *the few [e] who*, and generates overt *the some people who* alongside. Either both are generated (overgenerating the overt one, absent from iWeb's displayed lines) or a restriction on *some* under *the* blocks both (undergenerating *the some who*). That is §10's point, now with a source that commits to the structure.
  - [+Partitive] is the observation that the determinatives with independent uses are the ones with partitives, restated as a feature. It covers the facts ordinary Head states as use permissions; it doesn't derive them.
  - Every example in the chapter has an antecedent. The chapter has nothing on antecedentless uses.

## Kester 1996, "Adjectival inflection and the licensing of empty categories in DP", *JL* 32(1): 57–78 (DOI 10.1017/S0022226700000761)

- English allows null N only in the Human construction (*the rich*): "The only exception to this constraint is found in constructions as in (5)" (p. 60); "this specification corresponds to the only instance of N-pro that can be licensed in English" (p. 61); conclusion: "The only instance of N-pro we find in English is characterized by the features [+human, + generic, + plural]" (p. 77). The plural is stipulated: "the number specification of human generic pro in English must be stipulated as [+plural]" (p. 61 n. 5).
- Her arguments against nominalization (*\*a rich*, *\*two riches*, *\*Rich are lonely*, p. 60) are arguments that the word keeps its category.
- Dutch quantifiers (*enkelen*, *velen*, pp. 65–66): human reading with *-n*, and obligatory partitive *er* without a lexical noun. Out of scope, but it's her evidence that quantifiers aren't nominalized.
- Nothing on English determinatives beyond Lobeck-style ellipsis.
- **Bearing.** *The few who say X* is specific, not generic, so on her account it has to be antecedent ellipsis, and the *few would* data (82 of 94 NOW lines with nothing in view) apply. Cite for the claim and the counterexample, in two sentences.

## Llombart-Huesca 2002, "Anaphoric *one* and NP-ellipsis", *Studia Linguistica* 56(1): 59–89 (DOI 10.1111/1467-9582.00087)

- *One* is a last-resort support for an affix in NUM, like *do* (pp. 75–78). Empty NUM is licensed by strong agreement under immediate c-command; an intervening adjective blocks it (*many lazy ones*, pp. 78–79).
- Lobeck's licensing summarised, pp. 68–70 ([+plural], [+partitive], [+possessive]; *every* excluded as non-partitive).
- Identification needs an antecedent: "The elision of the lexical element the NP is free (only subject to identification conditions, that is, there must be an antecedent)" (p. 88).
- Independent singular demonstrative (*Give me that*): no NumP, and an empty NP identified as "'thing' ... with the word 'thing' used very loosely, including objects, facts, actions" (p. 82). That's an empty noun with general content under another name.
- The *one*/null complementarity (pp. 66–68: *many / some / three (\*ones) failed*, p. 67) is contradicted by Günther's COCA *two ones*, *several ones* (Günther 2018: 103).
- **Bearing.** Her account is antecedent ellipsis throughout, and she says so (p. 88). Her §2 arguments are against *one* being a noun, not for a null head against a non-null analysis.

## Günther 2018, "The rich, the poor, the obvious: Arguing for an ellipsis analysis of 'adjectives used as nouns'", in Leung & van der Wurff (eds.), *The Noun Phrase in English*, 77–112 (DOI 10.1075/la.246.04gun)

- **Target** is conversion (A→N): §2.3 shows the adjective keeps adjectival properties (no plural, grading, adverb modifiers), from p. 87.
- **Misfiles CGEL**, p. 79: among authors arguing for the ellipsis possibility, "Payne & Huddleston 2002 subsume them under the label 'fused-head'". CGEL rejects ellipsis for these (pp. 420–421).
- **Quantifiers without antecedent** get [+human]: *only two died* (p. 90), *as so many have feared* (p. 91); she notes CGEL (p. 411) treats this as a special fused head (p. 90 n. 6).
- **Her open problem**, pp. 106–107: *the* + silent noun + postmodifier is out (*the (\*ones) responsible for*, (87)), while *those responsible*, *many working in this domain*, *several who say* are fine. "I do not have a definite answer for this" (p. 107). Her account says the definite article doesn't license noun ellipsis (p. 106).
- **Content of the empty noun**, p. 108: content is retrieved from "grammatical and lexical information ... as well as the lexical meaning of the adjective and other elements, such as the verbal predicate"; and "Even though the claim that the empty noun is able to denote concepts is inconsistent with its being devoid of lexical content, we can adopt Olsen's reasoning that there are prototypical nominal concepts". Then, p. 109: "both are anaphoric".
- **Bearing.**
  - Her content comes from the overt word and the clause, which is what ordinary Head says (§3.2). The silent noun contributes a position and number/countability features.
  - "Both are anaphoric" (p. 109) denies the ellipsis/empty-noun split that §10 takes from Saab and Panagiotidis. §10 should note that the split is contested by one of its main practitioners, or stop depending on it.

## Across all four

Their positive arguments are mostly against conversion (Günther §2.3; Kester §§3, 5; Llombart-Huesca §2 on *one*). Ordinary Head isn't conversion; it keeps the category too. What's left is (a) parallels with antecedent ellipsis, (b) insertability of an overt noun (*the poor people*, *stuff*, *one*), and (c) licensing features that restate the lexical list. None of the four considers a category-keeping analysis without a null head except via CGEL's fusion, which Günther files with ellipsis.

## Open check: the genitive

§10 concedes that a silent noun explains the missing possessive (*\*some's*; Pullum 1975: 175–176). If antecedent-anaphoric fused heads, which are silent-headed on all four accounts, take the genitive freely, that explanation fails, and the gap belongs to independent determinatives and the generic Human construction specifically. **From memory, unverified:** *the latter's*, *the former's*, *the other's* are common. CGEL (p. 420) says fused heads "either cannot occur in the genitive at all or do so only very rarely" and lists *\*all's*, *\*many's*, *\*few's*, *\*some's*, with *each's* "occasionally attested". Proposed hand queries (COCA): `the latter 's`, `the former 's`, `the other 's`, controls `the poor 's`, `the rich 's`.
