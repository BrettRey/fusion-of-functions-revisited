## Open: §2, analyses and criterion (17 Oct)

- **L1, how far ordinary Head goes.** *Answered 17 Oct: version A. Version B is dropped from this paper and left to the taxonomic paper.* The old draft and the new §2 use two different versions of ordinary Head, and §6 has to pick one. Take *take some apples* and *take some*.

  **Version A**, the one §2 now draws (Figure 1c), is CGEL's tree with one link removed. In *take some apples* nothing changes: *some* is a determinative phrase (DP) functioning as determiner of the NP, as in CGEL. In *take some*, CGEL has that DP functioning both as determiner of the NP and as head of the nominal. Version A keeps it as head of the nominal and removes the determiner link.

  **Version B**, the old draft's (its figure comparing *take some apples* with *take some*, and its subsection "Phrase categories and structural constraints"), goes further. There, *some* builds an NP around itself the way a noun does, in every function. In *take some*, the word *some* heads the nominal directly, with no DP between them. In *take some apples*, the determiner is itself an NP, [NP [Nom [D *some*]]], the same category as *Kim's* in *Kim's apples*. That's what "the NP projection" means. Its payoff is a shorter list of the categories that can function as determiner: NP or PP, against CGEL's DP, NP or PP.

  Three things depend on the choice.
  1. §2 says ordinary Head "changes category in exactly the constructions where fusion does, at the same node". That's true of A and false of B, which also changes the category of the determiner in *some apples*, where fusion changes nothing. Keeping B means presenting it as a further step, with its own cost.
  2. The accepted plan for §6 (from muse's review of the paper's structure) gives a condition under which the analysis would be wrong, and it's written for B: "If determinative headed and genitive expressions need different structural constraints once lexical restrictions are held fixed, the shared projection advantage disappears." Under A, determinative and genitive determiners aren't the same category, so the condition has nothing to test. §6 would need another one. The natural candidate is the paper's criterion run in reverse: ordinary Head is wrong for a word if, standing alone, it shows some property of form that varies with the determiner function and not with the word. §3 looked for that in the genitive and didn't find it. The closest candidate is §4's condition on adjectives before the head (*the lucky few*, but not *lucky few* alone), which both analyses have to state.
  3. The old draft's section "If determinatives are nouns" is written on B: if determinatives are a kind of noun, the head of the nominal is always a noun. Under A the point survives in a weaker form, and it would go in §8 as a pointer to the taxonomic paper.

  **My recommendation is A, and dropping B from this paper.** §§2–4 are already written on A. This paper's claim is about functions, and B adds a claim about categories that the paper's criterion doesn't test. B pays off mainly if determinatives are nouns, which is the taxonomic paper's thesis. Dropping it costs a new condition of failure in §6 and a sentence in §8.

## Open: §4 and §6, pronouns and proper names (18 Oct)

**M1 now has its answer in the draft.** You asked for a proper accounting, with the cards shown whichever way they lie (18 Oct). It's in §4, after the treatment of *a lucky few*, and §6's table row and last two paragraphs point to it.

*Correction after the muse review (18 Oct, `reviews/muse-sec4-6-20261018-065227/TRIAGE.md`).* The bullets below record the first version, and two of them overclaim for ordinary Head.
- Question 1: the grammar does already ban pre-head dependents for pronouns. But ordinary Head's condition isn't the same statement, because it has to mention a determiner and *a*. So fusion's derivation saves a statement, though not a kind of statement the grammar would otherwise lack. The Matthews p. 10 sentence is cut, because it overread him.
- Question 2: two *every*-compounds are attested in the pronouns' exclamation. That's not enough to say that compounds with a personal base share the exception. Fusion's options now include a listed exception. The verdict is that the compounds need a listed exception on either analysis, and only on fusion does it go against a principle.

The first version's bullets:

- For ordinary Head: CGEL itself states the condition ordinary Head needs, for pronouns. They "do not normally allow internal pre-head dependents" (p. 430), and since pronouns have no determiner function, fusion can't derive that. CGEL derives the same gap from fusion only for the compounds such as *nothing* (p. 423). So what fusion's derivation saves is extending the pronoun condition to a second category, not having the condition at all. Matthews (2014: 10) treats *\*I met tall someone* the same way, as "a matter of 'position' only".
- For fusion: the old draft's main evidence, *the real me* and *the young Isaac Newton*, doesn't help ordinary Head. *The real you* takes *is*, not *are*, so the pronoun heads a singular count NP. CGEL would call it a common noun there (p. 429 and n. 46). Your taxonomic paper argues instead for a secondary use of the pronoun, on Halm's evidence that the reflexive keeps the pronoun's person (*The 12-year-old me is kicking myself*; `determinatives-as-nouns.tex` lines 838–840). §4 now gives both views and doesn't depend on either. Names take restrictive adjectives in secondary uses, where they select determiners "in the ordinary way" (pp. 520–521). In both cases the determiner is needed because the head is singular and count. That reasoning doesn't extend to plural *few*, which would be expected to work like *lucky survivors*, provided plural names do (M2, item 4).
- For ordinary Head, after M3: names and the core personal pronouns take a few non-restrictive adjectives with no determiner (*poor Henry*, *Lucky you!*). Simple determinatives don't (\**Lucky few!*), but compounds with a personal base do (*Poor everybody.*, *New Yorker* 2005; *Lucky you. Lucky everyone.*, *Everwood* 2004). Adjacency rules that out for fused determiner-heads, and CGEL says it's "not possible" (p. 423). Ordinary Head adds the compounds to CGEL's lexical exception.

### The question as asked on 17 Oct (superseded)

- **M1, should the argument from pronouns and proper names come back?** §6 says the missing adjective before a bare determinative (\**Lucky few escaped*, against *the lucky few*) comes nearest to the kind of evidence the paper's criterion asks for, and that fusion derives it from adjacency. It fails the criterion for one reason only: the adjective is also fine in *a lucky few*, where CGEL has the fused determiner-head *a few*.

  The old draft had a stronger reason, in its section "If determinatives are nouns". Pronouns and proper names behave the same way: they take a restrictive adjective before them only with a determiner (*the real me*, *the young Isaac Newton*; CGEL pp. 430, 520–521). So the grammar needs ordinary Head's condition for them in any case, and fusion's derivation saves nothing in what the grammar has to state. The argument doesn't need determinatives to be nouns, since the condition can be stated over heads that take no determiner in their usual use, whatever their category.

  It has a cost. Names and pronouns also take non-restrictive adjectives with no determiner (*Young Isaac Newton went off to Cambridge*, *Lucky you!*), and determinatives don't. Fusion's total exclusion fits that, and ordinary Head would have to state it. The old draft's COCA counts (350 tokens of *the real me*; 193 of 198 screened lines of *the real you* with the restrictive use) would need rechecking against the evidence repo.

  Should it go into §4, where the adjective is first weighed, or §6, or stay out?
